Family Watch Queue

A shared queue where everyone rates a show themselves, so the list surfaces what the whole family will actually agree on tonight.

SCOPE · DRAFT — awaiting your review

The Unique Kernel

Not one shared "we like this" score — your rating, his rating, theirs. Because ratings are per-person, the same data powers two views: a family view sorted by predicted agreement (tonight's safest bet), and a personal view for watching solo — shows you'd love that nobody else wants.

The Core Loop

The loop repeats every time the family needs an answer to "what should we watch?"

One specific family of three — mom, dad, kid — who watch TV together most evenings across Netflix, YouTube, Hulu, and Amazon Prime. Today: ideas land in the phone's Notes app, a plain list with no ratings, no platform info, no way to see who'd actually enjoy it.

Why This Matters

A cell biologist and longtime social entrepreneur (co-founder of Addgene, 22 years as CEO/CSO) who recently stepped back into an advisory role to make room for learning something new. This is a low-stakes way to learn AI-agent collaboration — while fixing a real evening friction point, turning "what do we watch" into genuine family time.

What "Working" Looks Like — the one-minute demo

  1. Add a show by pasting a Rotten Tomatoes link — genre/synopsis/rating/platform(s) auto-populate — then add a second show manually to show that path too.
  2. Each family member picks their name, sees which shows they haven't rated yet, and adds their own star/rank rating.
  3. The family view shows the list sorted with the most-agreed-upon show at the top — switch to the personal view and see a show she'd love that nobody else wants, perfect for solo exercise-watching.
  4. Mark watched or delete — both visible and working.

The Proof-of-Concept Boundary

  • Add a show: paste a Rotten Tomatoes link to auto-populate genre, synopsis, rating, and platform(s) when available — or enter everything manually, including platform, for anything not on Rotten Tomatoes or when RT doesn't list where to watch it
  • A show can have more than one platform (Netflix/YouTube/Hulu/Amazon Prime), since titles are often available in multiple places
  • Each of the three family members selects their name (no accounts/login) and gives their own star/rank rating
  • A way to see or filter which shows you personally haven't rated yet
  • Family queue view sorted by predicted agreement across everyone's ratings
  • Personal queue view for solo watching — surfaces shows rated highly by you but low by everyone else
  • Mark a show watched
  • Delete a show
  • Queue data stays private — kept out of the public code repo via .gitignore, same as API keys
  • Sharing the app with friends or other families beyond this household
  • Real user accounts / authentication Reason: unnecessary complexity for a closed group of three known people — a simple name picker covers it.
Inspiration & Identity
Directly inspired by Netflix's "My List" queue — but platform-agnostic, since this family watches across four different services. No specific visual reference yet; keep it simple and functional for now. Look and feel gets a proper pass in the PRD.
