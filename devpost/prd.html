GroupWatchlist

Everyone rates a show individually — the app surfaces what the family will agree on tonight, or what you'd enjoy watching alone.

The Core Journey

1

Open the app on your own phone — it already knows who you are, no re-entering your name.

2

Land on the Family view: the queue sorted by predicted agreement, with a tab to your Personal view and an "add new" button.

3

Empty queue? See a welcome message and a prompt to add the first show.

4

Tap "add new" — paste a Rotten Tomatoes link (auto-fills details) or go straight to manual entry. Either way, preview and edit before saving.

5

The new show appears at the bottom of the Family view — unrated, waiting for ratings.

6

Anyone taps in and adds their own 1–5 star rating.

7

The Family view re-sorts live: shows everyone's on board with rise to the top.

8

Watched it? Swipe → Mark Watched (moves to the archive). Nobody's into it? Swipe → Delete.

9

Watching solo (e.g. during exercise)? Switch to Personal view for a pick you'd love that the family isn't into.

The Kernel, Made Concrete

Same raw ratings, two different lenses — that's the whole idea.

Screens and Layout

Family View (landing)

Queue sorted by predicted agreement. Tab to Personal view, "add new" button, "unrated by me" toggle, small "Watched" link in the corner.

Personal View

Same row/detail pattern, filtered and sorted for solo picks.

Add Show

Rotten Tomatoes link or manual entry, side by side. Editable preview before saving.

Show Detail

Tap any row: platform(s), genre, synopsis, scores, link, and your rating control.

Watched Archive

Reached via the small corner link. Read-only, minimal — same basic info, no swipe actions, kept intentionally out of the way.

Look and Feel

Warm and clean / minimalist. Orange as the primary accent, with a complementary palette built around it — avoiding generic default AI-app styling.

Features and Behavior

Adding a Show
  • Two paths from the same first screen: paste a Rotten Tomatoes URL, or jump to manual entry.
  • Required: Rotten Tomatoes URL or title — nothing else.
  • Fields: title, cover image, platform(s) (multiple allowed), genre, synopsis, Tomatometer, Popcornmeter, link.
  • A successful link load shows an editable preview before saving — nothing saves silently.
  • A bad/unparseable link shows an error, lets you retry, and offers "add manual entry."
  • No image available (typically manual entries) → placeholder graphic.
Family View Sorting — the family half of the kernel

Score = lowest rating among whoever's rated it so far. Ties broken by average rating. Unrated shows sink to the bottom.

Personal View Sorting — the solo half of the kernel
  • Only shows you've rated appear.
  • Shows with zero ratings from anyone else are excluded — "unrated" isn't "unwanted."
  • Primary sort: your own rating, highest first (your 4–5★ shows before your lower-rated ones).
  • Within each tier: sorted by the lowest rating from everyone else, ascending — least-wanted-by-the-family bubbles up first.
Finding What You Haven't Rated
A toggle/filter on the Family view — "show only unrated by me" — instead of a separate screen, so you keep full context while working through new additions.
Marking Watched / Deleting
Swipe a row to reveal Delete and Mark Watched. Watched moves the show to the minimal read-only Watched Archive; Delete removes it entirely.
Main Row vs. Detail View
  • Row: title, cover image (or placeholder), each family member's star rating.
  • Detail: platform(s), genre, synopsis, Tomatometer/Popcornmeter, link, your rating control.

States and Boundaries

  • Empty state: "Welcome to your shared video queue! This is an easy way for you and your friends/family to share shows that you are interested in and decide on what to watch next." → "Start by adding your first movie or show."
  • Unrated show: bottom of Family view; never appears in any Personal view.
  • Partially-rated show: sorts normally, using only the ratings that exist.
  • Rotten Tomatoes link error: error message, retry, or jump to manual entry.
  • Watched: moved to the minimal Watched Archive.
  • Deleted: gone, no archive.
  • Persistence: identity remembered per device; queue data persists between sessions and stays private, kept out of the public repo.

Proof-of-Concept Boundary

  • Pick-your-name-once identity, remembered per device
  • Add via Rotten Tomatoes link (editable preview) or manual entry, incl. multi-platform, cover image, genre, synopsis, both RT scores, link field
  • Link-error handling with manual-entry fallback
  • 1–5 star rating per person per show
  • Family view: min-rating sort, average tiebreak, unrated at bottom
  • Personal view: your-high / others'-low sort, excludes zero-rated-by-others
  • "Unrated by me" toggle
  • Swipe → Delete / Mark Watched
  • Minimal read-only Watched Archive
  • Empty-state welcome copy
  • Warm, orange-based visual direction
  • Sharing the queue with friends or other families beyond this household The empty-state copy says "friends/family" aspirationally, but this build only supports the 3 known household members.
  • A richer, browsable/searchable Watched Archive Kept to a minimal read-only list for now.
  • Automatic updates if a show's platform availability changes after adding Not needed to prove the kernel.
  • Real user accounts / authentication Unnecessary for a closed group of three known people — a per-device name pick covers it.
  • Switching identities mid-session on a shared device Not needed — each family member has their own phone.
Product Decisions & Why
  • Individual ratings, not one shared score — powers both the family-agreement view and the personal-picks view from the same data.
  • Remember identity per device — everyone has their own phone, so no re-entry or mid-session switching needed.
  • Family score = minimum, not average — "everyone needs to be on board," so one low rating should sink a show.
  • Personal view excludes zero-rated-by-others shows — "nobody's rated it" isn't evidence the family doesn't want it.
  • Watched archive stays minimal — a record exists, but it shouldn't clutter the main queue.
  • A toggle, not a third screen, for unrated-by-me — less to build, same context retained.
Open Questions
Exactly how the Rotten Tomatoes link gets parsed — what data is actually available to pull programmatically, and how reliably — is a technical feasibility question for 4-spec, not a product one. Flagged for spec to investigate before locking in the exact field list.
